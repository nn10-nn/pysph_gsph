1. Introduction

   The generalized Riemann problem (GRP) scheme is a second-order accurate Godunov method in time and space and was
originally devised by Ben-Artzi and Falcovitz [3] for one-dimensional (1D) system of the non-relativistic ﬂuid ﬂow by using a
piecewise linear function to approximate the initial data and then analytically solving a local GRP at each cell interface so as
to yield numerical ﬂuxes. Then the GRP scheme was extensively applied to a large variety of wave conﬁgurations in multi-
dimensional problems, including the reactive ﬂow [7,2], compressible ﬂows with moving boundaries [14], etc. The compre-
hensive description can be found in [4] and references therein. The derivation of the GRP scheme has two versions: the
Lagrangian and the Eulerian. The latter is always derived by using the former. The main advantage of using the Lagrangian
framework is that the contact discontinuity in each local wave pattern is always ﬁxed with speed zero and the rarefaction
waves and/or shock waves are located on either side. However, the passage from the Lagrangian coordinate to the Eulerian is
sometimes quite delicate, particularly for sonic cases and multi-dimensional applications. In order to efﬁciently deal with the
difﬁculty, a direct Eulerian GRP scheme was developed for the shallow water equations [21] and for hyperbolic balance laws
[6,5], which used the main ingredient of Riemann invariants to decompose the strong coupling of nonlinear waves into a
form of their simple superposition so that the rarefaction waves could be analytically resolved in a quite straightforward

a few mesh points and reduce possible errors or oscillations. Recently, the direct Eulerian GRP scheme was successfully ex-

    Relativistic hydrodynamics (RHD) plays a major role in many ﬁelds of modern physics e.g. astrophysics, high-energy and
nuclear physics, etc. To simulate ﬂows near massive objects it would be necessary to modify the equations further to include
the local curvature of space–time or the relativistic effect. The dynamics of the relativistic system requires solving highly
nonlinear equations, rendering the analytic treatment of practical problems extremely difﬁcult. Wilson ﬁrst attempted to
solve the general RHD equations in an Eulerian form by using the common method of ﬁrst-order backward space-differenc-
ing technique and the Richtmyer–von Neumann artiﬁcial viscosity techniques for treating the convective terms in the equa-
tions of motion [29,30]. After that, various shock-capturing methods with the exact or approximate Riemann solvers were
developed for the RHDs. The two-shock approximation of Colella was extended to the relativistic regime in [1]. A similar
work was given in [9], where all waves emanating from an initial discontinuity were treated as themselves discontinuous,
in other words, the jump conditions are also approximated used for the rarefaction waves. The Roe solver was developed for
the RHDs in [13]. The HLL (Harten–Lax–van Leer–Einfeldt) method and the ﬂux corrected transport algorithm in the context
of the RHD equations were studied in [25,12]. An extension of the HLLC (Harten–Lax–van Leer-Contact) approximate Rie-
mann solver for the RHDs was presented in [23]. A ﬂux-splitting method based on the spectral decomposition was extended
to the RHDs in [11]. ENO (essentially non-oscillatory) based methods for the RHD system were studied in [10,32,28]. The
piecewise parabolic method was proposed in [24]. An adaptive moving mesh method is recently developed for the 2D
RHD equations in [17]. The readers are also referred to the early review article [22].
    The paper is concerned with an extension of the GRP scheme for the 1D RHDs developed in [31] to the 2D RHD equations.
The key step is to resolve the GRP of the split 2D RHD equations at each cell interface. An important difference from the GRP
of the 1D RHD equations as well as the non-relativistic hydrodynamical equations comes from a purely multi-dimensional
relativistic feature that the ﬂow regions across the shock or rarefaction wave in the GRP of the split 2D RHD equations are
nonlinearly coupled through the Lorentz factor which is also built in terms of the tangential velocities.
    The paper is organized as follows. Section 2 introduces some preliminaries and notations including several forms of the
governing equations of the RHDs and the Riemann invariants as well as their total differentials. Section 3 develops the GRP
scheme, whose outline is given in Section 3.1. The resolution of the (local) GRP of the split 2D RHD equations is presented in
Section 3.2, where the rarefaction wave and the shock wave are resolved by using the Riemann invariants and the Rankine–
Hugoniot jump conditions in Sections 3.2.1 and 3.2.2, respectively. As soon as they are done, the limiting values of the time
derivatives of the solutions at the singularity point can be calculated (see Section 3.2.3). A special and simple case, the so-
called acoustic case, is discussed in Section 3.2.4. The numerical experiments will be conducted in Section 4. Section 5 con-
cludes the paper with several remarks.

5. Conclusions

    This paper proposed a two-dimensional (2D) extension of the direct Eulerian generalized Riemann problem (GRP) scheme
for one-dimensional (1D) special relativistic hydrodynamics (RHD) in [31]. In order to derive the direct Eulerian GRP scheme,
the (local) GRP of the split 2D RHD equations in the Eulerian formulation was directly resolved by using corresponding Rie-
mann invariants and Rankine–Hugoniot jump conditions. The ﬂow regions across the shock or rarefaction wave in the GRP of
the split 2D RHD equations are nonlinearly coupled through the Lorentz factor which is also built in terms of the tangential
velocities. It is a purely multi-dimensional relativistic feature and an important difference from the GRP of the 1D RHD equa-
tions as well as the non-relativistic hydrodynamical equations. Several numerical examples have been given to demonstrate
the accuracy and effectiveness of the proposed GRP scheme. The extension of the direct Eulerian GRP scheme to the four-
